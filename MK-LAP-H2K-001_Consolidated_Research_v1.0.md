## 01 | Executive research finding

Across the selected mouse developmental studies, WNT9B-associated outcomes depend on experimental and developmental context. Carroll establishes an early WNT9B–WNT4 inductive relationship; Kobayashi demonstrates SIX2-dependent progenitor maintenance and WNT9B-dependent ectopic differentiation, subject to compound-mutant limitations; Karner establishes later planar-cell-polarity-associated tubular morphogenesis, while convergent extension remains a proposed mechanistic explanation.

**None of these studies establishes adult human nephron-regenerative sufficiency.**

| Distinct process | Primary evidence | Not demonstrated |

| Progenitor maintenance / differentiation | Kobayashi 2008 | A complete linear WNT9B–SIX2–WNT4 pathway |
| Later tubular morphogenesis | Karner 2009 | Direct live observation of convergent-extension movements |

## 03 | Carroll et al. (2005): early induction

**Reference:** Carroll TJ et al. *Wnt9b plays a central role in the regulation of mesenchymal to epithelial transitions underlying organogenesis of the mammalian urogenital system.* Developmental Cell. DOI: [10.1016/j.devcel.2005.05.016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.devcel.2005.05.016).

### Models, genotypes and developmental stages

Developing mouse urogenital tissue and embryonic mesenchymal induction assays; `Wnt9b` loss-of-function and `Wnt4`-deficient responding tissue are included. The study describes conditional `Wnt9b` allele construction and loss-of-function derivatives. Precise allele-to-panel mapping remains pending direct panel verification. Reported expression contexts include E9.5 Wolffian duct, E11.0 ureteric regions, and E11.5 developing ureteric structures. Mesonephric histology is reported at E11.5, with Pax2-associated comparisons at E10.5.

### Reported 72-hour induction-assay frequencies

| Inducing system | Responding tissue | Induction |
|---|---|---:|
| Spinal cord | Wild-type mesenchyme | 5/5* |
| NIH3T3-LacZ | Wild-type mesenchyme | 0/12* |
| L-cells | Wild-type mesenchyme | 0/22* |
| NIH3T3-Wnt4 | Wild-type mesenchyme | 11/11 |
| NIH3T3-Wnt4 | `Wnt9b`-null mesenchyme | 9/10 |
| NIH3T3-Wnt9b | Wild-type mesenchyme | 1/31 |
| L-Wnt9b | Wild-type mesenchyme | 26/26 |
| L-Wnt9b | `Wnt4`-null mesenchyme | 0/9 |

\* Control counts retained from prior extraction; direct confirmation of remaining controls is still outstanding. Five principal counts (11/11, 9/10, 1/31, 26/26, 0/9) were reconciled in the preceding review.

### Interpretation and limitations

`Wnt4`-producing cells induced tubules in `Wnt9b`-null mesenchyme, whereas `Wnt9b`-producing cells failed to induce tubules in `Wnt4`-null mesenchyme in the reported assay. This supports direct partial ordering: **WNT9B acts upstream of WNT4 in the studied induction process**.

The NIH3T3-Wnt9b 1/31 versus L-Wnt9b 26/26 comparison demonstrates producing-cell-system dependence, but neither identifies the responsible molecular property nor demonstrates a difference in responding-cell populations. Tubule induction in an embryonic assay is not adult functional nephron replacement.

## 04 | Kobayashi et al. (2008): progenitor competence

**Reference:** Kobayashi A et al. *Six2 defines and regulates a multipotent self-renewing nephron progenitor population throughout mammalian kidney development.* Cell Stem Cell. DOI: [10.1016/j.stem.2008.05.020](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.stem.2008.05.020).

Mouse genetic lineage and loss-of-function evidence supports maintenance of a SIX2-associated nephron-progenitor population and multipotency in at least some SIX2-expressing cells. Loss of SIX2 activity permits ectopic nephron-tubule contribution dependent on WNT9B inductive signaling.

| **12.5 dpc** | Histology and laminin/cytokeratin analysis |
| **Renal vesicles** | Not observed in `Wnt9b−/−` or compound mutant |
| **Critical confounding features** | Retarded ureteric-bud ingrowth; severe reduction of Pax2-positive cap mesenchyme in compound mutants |

The compound-mutant findings support WNT9B dependence of the ectopic differentiation phenotype, but simultaneous abnormalities in ureteric-bud development and cap mesenchyme prevent interpretation as a clean, isolated linear pathway. This study does not demonstrate adult-human nephron-progenitor restoration or regenerative sufficiency.

## 06 | Cross-study mechanistic reconciliation

| Evidence dimension | Carroll | Kobayashi | Karner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Developmental focus** | Early induction | Progenitor maintenance and ectopic differentiation | Later tubule morphogenesis |
| **Direct relationship** | WNT9B upstream of WNT4 | SIX2-loss phenotype requires WNT9B signal | WNT9B-associated polarity and division defects |
| **Contextual factor** | Producing-cell system | Responding progenitor state | Developmental stage and tissue architecture |
| **Major limitation** | Induction ≠ mature function | Compound-mutant confounding | Convergent extension proposed |

The studies support context-dependent WNT9B-associated outcomes but do not comprise a single factorial experiment independently isolating producing-system context, progenitor state, stage, and organization. Shared molecular involvement must not be treated as proof of one complete linear mechanism.

## 08 | Alternative explanations and inference limits

Adult human failure to regenerate lost nephrons could involve loss of relevant progenitor populations, altered responsiveness, missing spatial tissue relationships, or multiple simultaneous restrictions. These selected mouse studies do not distinguish which explanation dominates in adult humans.

Three evidence levels must remain separate: cellular competence, structural organization, and sustained physiological function. A developmental marker is not an organized nephron; an organized precursor is not proof of filtration or long-term integration.

## 12 | Final scientific disposition

The selected mouse developmental studies establish experimentally distinguishable WNT9B-associated roles in early induction, SIX2-regulated progenitor differentiation, and later tubular morphogenesis. They support a context-dependent interpretation, not a universal regeneration mechanism. Convergent extension remains a mechanistic proposal in Karner. Adult human nephron-regenerative sufficiency and clinical efficacy are not established.

WNT9B may initiate developmental signaling, SIX2 helps regulate progenitor maintenance, and WNT4 participates in nephron induction. But successful regeneration would require coordinated cellular competence, tissue organization, integration, and sustained physiological function.

**Signal response ≠ cellular competence ≠ organized nephron ≠ sustained renal function.** These are distinct evidentiary milestones, not biologically independent processes or an experimentally established sequence sufficient to produce regeneration.

**Disposition:** Scientifically defensible as a bounded synthesis restricted to the selected mouse developmental studies; establishes no adult-human regenerative mechanism.
